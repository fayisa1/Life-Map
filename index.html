Fitness habits
What each habit in your Fitness branch means and why it matters.
Movement (walk, cardio)
Steady activity that raises your heart rate. It strengthens the heart and lungs, burns energy and lifts mood. Auto-ticks when you log Cardio, Walk or Sport in Track.
Meals, mostly whole foods
Regular meals built from minimally processed food. They steady energy and blood sugar and supply protein, fibre and vitamins. Auto-ticks when every main meal is eaten and about two-thirds are marked whole foods.
Water goal
Drinking your daily glasses. Water supports focus, digestion and temperature control; even mild dehydration brings headaches and tiredness. Auto-ticks when you reach your glass goal.
Sleep
Enough sleep for your age. Memory, immunity, mood and muscle recovery all happen during sleep. The target adapts to your date of birth.
Bedtime kept
Going to bed at your planned time. A steady schedule trains your body clock so sleep comes easier and Fajr is less of a struggle.
Daily quiet, 10 min
Ten minutes of dhikr, reflection or a slow walk with no screen. It lowers stress and trains attention.
Screen breaks
Standing, stretching and looking into the distance for a minute each hour. It eases eye strain and a stiff neck and back, and breaks up long sitting.
Strength session
Resistance work with your body, bands or weights. It builds and keeps muscle and bone, protects joints and helps metabolism. Aim for 2 to 3 a week; auto-ticks from Strength.
Mobility, 10 min
Gentle stretching and joint movement. It keeps range of motion, eases stiffness and lowers injury risk. Target 5 days a week.
Screens off before bed
No phone or TV in the last stretch before sleep. Bright light and stimulating content delay sleepiness. Target 5 nights a week.
Talked to someone
A real conversation with a friend, relative or counsellor. Social connection protects mental health. Target once a week.
Rest day
A planned day with no hard training, only an easy walk or stretching. Muscles and nerves adapt during rest, which prevents burnout and injury.
Exercise types
The workout types you can pick in Track, and what each is for.
Upper body
Chest, back, shoulders and arms (push-ups, rows, presses). Builds pushing and pulling strength for daily tasks and posture. Counts as Strength.
Lower body
Legs and hips (squats, lunges, bridges, calf raises). The biggest muscles, so it builds strength, balance and bone density. Counts as Strength.
Full body
A mix of upper, lower and core in one session. Efficient when time is short. Counts as Strength.
Cardio
Sustained effort such as running, cycling or intervals. Improves stamina and heart health. Counts toward Movement.
Walk
A brisk 30 to 40 minute walk. Gentle on joints, easy to repeat daily and good for mood. Counts toward Movement.
Sport or hike
Play, long walks or hikes. Mixes cardio with fun and company, which helps you keep going. Counts toward Movement.
Mobility
Stretching and joint circles (cat-cow, hip openers, twists). Keeps you flexible and pain-free, and works well after training.
Rest day
Easy walking and light stretching only. Recovery is when the training effect actually happens.
General information, not medical advice.
Meals and nutrition
What each meal and nutrition tick in Track means and what it does for you.
Breakfast
The first meal of the day. Protein plus a slow carbohydrate (eggs, oats, foul, yogurt, fruit) gives steady energy and fewer cravings.
Lunch
The main midday meal: protein, vegetables and a slow carbohydrate. It carries you through the afternoon without a crash.
Dinner
Lighter than lunch (protein and vegetables). A lighter evening meal sits easier and helps sleep.
Snack
A small planned bite such as nuts, fruit or yogurt. It bridges long gaps so you do not overeat at the next meal.
Suhoor
The pre-dawn meal when fasting. Slow carbohydrates, protein and water keep you full and hydrated through the day.
Iftar
The meal that ends the fast. Start gently with dates and water, then a balanced meal, to restore fluid and blood sugar.
Whole foods
Foods close to their natural state: grains, legumes, vegetables, fruit, eggs, plain meat and fish. They bring fibre and nutrients that processed food loses.
Protein
Meat, fish, eggs, dairy, beans or lentils. It repairs muscle, keeps you full and supports immunity.
Veg or fruit
Plants rich in fibre, vitamins and minerals. They aid digestion and lower long-term risk of heart disease.
5 portions of fruit and veg
Five handfuls a day. A simple target that raises fibre and micronutrient intake.
Protein at every meal
Spreading protein across meals helps muscle repair and steadies appetite.
No sugary drinks
Sodas and sweet juices add sugar fast without filling you. Water or unsweetened tea avoids energy spikes and crashes.
No fried or fast food
Heavy frying and fast food add excess fat, salt and calories. Cooking simply at home keeps intake in check.
Ate slowly, screens off
It takes about 20 minutes to feel full. Eating slowly without screens helps you stop at enough.
Last meal 2+ hours before bed
Gives digestion time to settle, reduces reflux and improves sleep quality.
General information, not medical advice.

Location
Qibla
4.7° N
True bearing from north, clockwise. With declination +0° the magnetic compass reads 4.7°.
About 1,579 km to the Kaaba. Geodesic on the WGS84 ellipsoid, Kaaba at 21.422487°, 39.826206°.
Phone compasses read magnetic north. Enter your local declination (search “magnetic declination” for your city) so the live compass turns it into true north. Hold the phone flat, keep it away from magnets and metal, and wave it in a figure 8 to calibrate.
Sun method (no compass needed)
Sun: 107° bearing, 3° up. Face the sun, then turn 102° to your left. That is Qibla.
Works in daylight when the sun is not overhead. It uses the sun’s computed position for your location and the time on your phone, so it is not affected by magnets or declination.
